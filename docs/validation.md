# Assessment and matching validation protocol

**Status: the candidate assessment has a synthetic validation study with reported results (below); matching and FSP parts remain a protocol, not a validation claim.** The requirements call for a candidate assessment grade that cannot be forcibly downgraded, a time-limited grade change, ranking informed by verified achievements, and evidence of expert validity, discrimination, reliability and top-k pair quality. The requirements PDF was provided separately from this checkout; this document uses its extracted requirements summary. The following synthetic expectations are **test oracles**, not measured outcomes or claims about real candidates/FSP credentials.

## Candidate assessment: mechanism and results

**Results: [`validation/assessment-results.md`](validation/assessment-results.md)** (Russian report, regenerated by `cd apps/api && gradle validateAssessment`; raw numbers in [`assessment-results.json`](validation/assessment-results.json)). The study simulates 10 000 synthetic candidates through the production engine and compares it with the previous five-question test on the same simulees: accuracy against reference labels, macro-F1 and confusion matrix; test–retest agreement on independently generated item instances (weighted kappa, θ correlation); per-family p-values, item–rest correlations and information; conditional bias/SD of θ̂ across forms; instance overlap between candidates and a memorization attack with leaks of 200/1 000/5 000 served questions; sensitivity to the decision margin, imperfect isomorphism and test length. Item parameters are expert-assigned priors, so the numbers describe the mechanism under its assumptions; section 9 of the report gives the calibration plan (pilot, IRT fit, instance variance, expert-labelled sample, drift and leak monitoring).

- **Content** ([`assessment/content`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/assessment/content)): item *families* — one skill at one calibrated difficulty with 2PL parameters (*a*, *b*; multiple choice adds a fixed guessing floor *c* = 1/options). Most families are parametric generators (SQL over generated tables, code tracing in Kotlin/Python/JS, event loop, CSS specificity, statistics, unit economics, WCAG contrast, …); the rest are sets of equivalent scenario variants. Every attempt gets its own instance drawn from a CSPRNG seed; the key is stored only in `assessment_items` and never serialized. The legacy `questions` bank (V1/V2) is no longer served.
- **Test** ([`Cat.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/assessment/Cat.kt), [`Irt.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/assessment/Irt.kt)): survey (industry, specialization, experience, team roles, work format) → declared grade → 12 adaptive items in 30 minutes. Item choice: maximum Fisher information at the routing estimate within topic quotas of the blueprint, least-exposed among near-equal candidates. Scoring: EAP with N(0, 1) prior (the declaration only routes the test). Cut-points on θ: middle −0.5, senior +0.8 (junior entry −1.5); a declared grade is confirmed when θ̂ − k·SE ≥ cut (k chosen by the sweep in the report); score = 100·Φ(θ̂). An expired attempt counts unanswered items as wrong and never confirms a grade.
- **Grade rules** ([`RetakePolicy.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/assessment/RetakePolicy.kt)): confirmed grade = max(previous, declared if passed), first failure floors at junior, never lowered. Per specialization, one grade change per 90 days; immediate exceptions: after a failure one attempt one level lower, after a confident pass one attempt one level higher (not if that level was failed in the window). Enforced at attempt start (`409` with `retakeAvailableAt`).
- **Vacancy blueprint** ([`Blueprint.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/assessment/Blueprint.kt)): ru/en skill dictionary with light stemming turns a vacancy text into topic weights (60 % vacancy, 40 % specialization core) and item quotas; `POST /api/assessments/blueprint` shows skills, quotas and freshly generated sample items for the five demo vacancies or any text.
- **Checks run**: unit tests for every generator (determinism, option uniqueness, keys re-derived from rendered text by independent oracles on hundreds of seeds), IRT/EAP, retake rules, blueprint and CAT selection; a DB test runs survey → failed senior → immediate middle → retake blocks → expiry against PostgreSQL. [`smoke_assessment.py`](smoke_assessment.py) repeats that path over HTTP against the running stack.

## What exists now and what was actually checked (matching, profile, invitations)

- V2 requires `processingConsent=true` separately from `consentPublication=true` to store a profile and start an assessment; matches require both consents, a nonblank full name, same specialization, a completed assessment and assessed grade meeting the effective minimum. `DELETE /api/candidates/me/consent` clears profile fields and erases the candidate's attempts/answers, achievements, resume drafts and invitations while leaving an empty account/profile shell. `GET /api/assessments/history` lists the candidate's completed attempts with survey, declared grade, θ, SE, score and confirmed grade, newest first. [`ResumeRoutes.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/ResumeRoutes.kt) accepts one text-extractable PDF at most 5 MB/30 pages with processing consent, stores a suggested-fields draft rather than directly applying parsed content, then requires candidate review/confirmation within one day; confirmation updates selected profile fields, never verified email or publication consent. Do not upload real resumes for the synthetic protocol. `GET /api/candidates/me/resume.pdf` produces a branded Cyrillic PDF from a consented saved profile; it does not issue an official credential.
- Matches require publication consent, processing consent, a nonblank full name, same specialization, a completed assessment and assessed grade meeting the employer's effective minimum. Rank is `selected assessment score + min(sum of verified achievement points, 20) + 5 for equal nonblank industry`, descending, then candidate UUID ascending. Achievement records have server-side `verified_at NOT NULL`; there is no candidate achievement-submission endpoint. The route returns the entire sorted list, **not a paginated top-k endpoint**. Optional `specialization` and `grade` query parameters override employer-need defaults; comma-separated `techStack` requires all requested skills case-insensitively, while `verifiedAchievements=true` requires at least one server-stored verified achievement. `techStack` is self-reported and filters results but contributes no points. `desiredGrade` is survey intent, not the assessed grade.
- Executed here (earlier run, before the adaptive assessment; current assessment checks are listed in the section above): `cd apps/api && gradle test --console=plain` returned `BUILD SUCCESSFUL`; the generated JUnit reports show **8 unit tests passed** (4 validation + 4 resume) without a DB. A second run with `TEST_DATABASE_URL=jdbc:postgresql://localhost:5432/fsp_career` against the local Compose PostgreSQL returned **9 passed, 0 skipped, 0 failed**, including the Flyway/seed/invalid-role constraint integration check; the report was inspected after that run. [`ValidationTest.kt`](../apps/api/src/test/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/ValidationTest.kt) checks input-grade/text validation, salary request deserialization and default invitation contact fields. [`ResumeTest.kt`](../apps/api/src/test/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/ResumeTest.kt) tests extraction of synthetic PDF suggestions, PDF-size/type rejection, career-field validation and Cyrillic PDF export, not HTTP upload/confirm authorization or PDF malware safety. [`DatabaseIntegrationTest.kt`](../apps/api/src/test/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/DatabaseIntegrationTest.kt) checked the 25-question V1+V2 seed count and invalid-role constraint against the local database. These tests do **not** exercise the HTTP assessment/ranking/authorization flows or psychometric quality. A real local database migration/constraint check ran. A host-run Ktor server backed by that database returned `GET /health` 200, `GET /openapi.yaml` 200, unauthenticated `GET /api/candidates/me` 401 and an allowed-origin (`http://localhost:3000`) CORS preflight 200. Separately, [`smoke_e2e.py`](<smoke_e2e.py>) passed against all six running Compose services: it creates short-lived **admin-verified synthetic** users and a temporary direct-grant test client, checks real JWT issuer/audience/role enforcement and rejects a tampered signature or newly issued token missing the API audience, processing/publication consent, an assessment attempt and immediate retake rejection (now the survey + adaptive flow), matching and skill filtering, reviewed multipart résumé import and branded PDF export, pre-acceptance contact masking, accepted-invitation contact release, and consent revocation/removal from matches. Its DB-inserted outcomes and achievements **are not evidence** that the assessment scores responses or that FSP has verified awards. [`smoke_assessment.py`](<smoke_assessment.py>) (rewritten for the adaptive test, see above) checks the survey → declared grade → lower-grade path over HTTP. These are local synthetic checks, not measured matching effectiveness or security/fairness accreditation.

1. Use a **throwaway PostgreSQL database**, API and Keycloak realm, never real people or production data. Pin repository revision, JDK/Gradle/PostgreSQL versions, DB migration checksum, time zone UTC, API configuration, issued synthetic role-bearing tokens and execution time. 2. Create distinct synthetic identities: candidate **A** (`backend`, industry `fintech`, opted in), candidate **B** (`backend`, `fintech`, opted in), candidate **C** (`backend`, not opted in), candidate **D** (`frontend`, opted in), candidate **E** (`backend`, opted in, no assessment), and employer **H** (`backend`, `fintech`, minimum `junior`). Set `processingConsent=true` for A/B/C/D/E; grant `consentPublication` only where explicitly opted in. Give each a nonblank synthetic name; keep contact addresses fictitious and private. Record UUIDs locally, never in public result dumps. Establish completed attempts via legitimate API calls in the isolated instance; because item instances are generated per attempt and the server holds the key, record **actual** attempt results rather than assuming a particular draw. For deterministic boundary tests, fixture preparation may insert completed synthetic attempts into test DB in a transaction, with explicit `submitted_at`, `specialization`, `target_grade`, `passed`, `score` and `grade`, solely in that isolated DB; separately validate the HTTP scoring path with normal API submissions. Do not call DB-inserted outcomes evidence that the assessment correctly scored responses.
3. **Grade/retake matrix:** covered by `AssessmentFlowDatabaseTest` and `smoke_assessment.py` (all-wrong senior attempt → not passed, junior floor, only middle allowed; all-correct middle attempt → middle confirmed, senior blocked for 90 days; expired attempt closed as failed; foreign/invalid/out-of-order answers rejected). For score-boundary cases inject completed attempts into the isolated DB only and label them as fixtures.
4. **Ranking matrix:** prepare same-specialization assessed A/B with equal scores and industries; verify tie order by UUID. Give A two server-inserted verified achievements worth 12 and 15 points: expect `achievementScore=20`, never 27. Give B no achievement; for the industry test change B's industry to `retail` and H's industry to `retail` while A remains `fintech`: expect B's industry bonus 5 in the explanation, all other score components unchanged. Vary score gaps to cover bonus win/loss/tie, thresholds (`minGrade` middle/senior), C non-publication, D different specialization and E unassessed; C/D/E must not appear with H's `backend` defaults. Check `specialization=frontend` finds D only if assessed; `grade=senior` overrides the need, `techStack=Kotlin,SQL` requires both skills irrespective of casing, and `verifiedAchievements=true` excludes B. Supply oversized/invalid query values and check `400`. Self-reported tech stack should never change total score. Reset filter/fixture states between assertions. Candidate-supplied `grade`, `score`, or an invented achievement in profile JSON must not increase rank or create an achievement row; verify returned profile and database state. The schema cannot store `verified_at=NULL`, so an unverified-achievement ranking test needs a *proposed* pending-submission schema, not a fabricated current-row fixture. Capture complete `/api/matches` ordering, explanatory terms and UUID tiebreak; slice the first k client-side for benchmark purposes and state that this is **not** a server top-k API.
5. **Privacy/security:** bearer absent, bad signature, expired token, wrong issuer/audience, wrong role, foreign candidate attempt, foreign invitation, and candidate-as-employer should fail without data disclosure. Check that `/api/matches` never contains email/phone and that invitation GET/POST returns null contacts before acceptance; only an invited candidate can accept and then both authorized parties can see contact. Review rejection/viewing transitions and logs/errors for leakage. Check email ownership separately: the API derives candidate email from a Keycloak token with `email_verified=true` and rejects mismatched nonblank profile email; test both verified and unverified tokens. Check `PUT` without processing consent fails, and independent publication withdrawal removes discovery without erasing a stored profile. For PDF import, test upload without consent, wrong owner/expired/already-confirmed draft, malformed/encrypted/oversize/non-text PDF and parsed email spoofing; verify only reviewed fields are applied, with no email/publication changes. Delete processing consent and verify profile fields cleared, attempts/history, draft and achievements erased and invitations absent for both parties; verify no match or new invitation remains. This deletes application rows but does not establish complete erasure of backups, Keycloak account, logs or downstream copies; audit those separately. Local Mailpit handles only synthetic development verification, not proof of delivery to real mailboxes or a production security certification.
6. Store a redacted run manifest (commit/revision, migration digest, fixture SQL digest, UTC dates, environment versions), HTTP status/response assertions, test report counts and anonymized ranking output. Destroy the throwaway database after review. If result differs from expected, preserve failing fixture and classify implementation bug versus oracle error. Never publish answers, tokens, contact data, or a real candidate ranking.

## Independent quality study before any consequential use

**Assessment quality (content, discrimination, reliability, decision consistency).** The synthetic study and the real-data calibration plan are in [`validation/assessment-results.md`](validation/assessment-results.md) (sections 5–9). Before consequential use: a pilot with linear forms and anchor families, an IRT fit per specialization, instance-level variance per family, a blinded expert-labelled sample to check agreement with the reference grades, DIF review, and continuous drift/leak monitoring per family.

**Top-k expert/pair evaluation.** For each held-out employer need, have independent domain judges label *all* eligible synthetic/consented candidates (or a predefined probability sample covering retrieved and unretrieved candidates) for relevance 0–3 and pairwise preference; adjudicate or retain ties/uncertainty. Freeze system version, `k` (e.g. 3 and 5), candidate pool and exclusions before labels are revealed. Evaluate eligibility precision and recall, `precision@k`, `recall@k` against judged-relevant candidates, `nDCG@k` with explicit gain/discount, and pair concordance: among judge-comparable candidate pairs involving at least one system top-k candidate, count system-correct order / all comparable evaluated pairs, excluding expert ties and reporting tie/unknown counts. Report macro average over employer needs, number of needs/candidates/pairs, bootstrap confidence intervals resampling needs, and comparison against score-only and randomized eligible baselines under the same pools. If not all eligible pairs are judged, report judged-pair coverage and sampling weights; never treat unjudged candidates as irrelevant. Separate offline agreement from hiring utility and fairness. **No such judgments, samples, metric values or baselines are available in this repository.**

## Proposed FSP integration boundary (not implemented)

Use an issuer-controlled connector/service account, not candidate-entered points. Proposed `achievement_verifications(id, candidate_id, source='fsp', external_subject_ref, external_achievement_id, achievement_type, issuer, achieved_at, status[pending|verified|revoked], verified_at, revoked_at, evidence_ref, source_version, last_checked_at)` with a uniqueness constraint on `(source,external_achievement_id,candidate_id)`; candidate-to-external-subject binding must be consented and issuer-validated. Keep evidence references restricted, provenance/audit events immutable, and avoid copying sensitive payloads to rank responses. A separately versioned scoring policy maps verified, unrevoked types to capped points; replay-safe imports, revocation refresh, manual review, appeal/correction and consent withdrawal update eligibility. Explicitly distinguish participation, prize and qualification and obtain FSP approval for use of marks/claims. Existing [`achievements`](../apps/api/src/main/resources/db/migration/V1__career.sql) have title, issuer, points and mandatory verified timestamp but no issuer proof, revocation, source ID or candidate identity binding; do not label their rows official FSP achievements without external verification.

## Decision and limitations

The current prototype supports demonstration of intended mechanics, **not** a validated selection instrument. The assessment has a synthetic validation study with expert-assigned item parameters; real calibration, employer agreement and top-k matching quality are not yet measured. Keep synthetic demonstrations separate from user-facing credential claims; require the functional security matrix, expert study and independent held-out ranking evaluation before claiming fitness for recruitment decisions.
