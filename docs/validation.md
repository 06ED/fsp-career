# Assessment and matching validation protocol

**Status: protocol, not a validation claim.** The requirements supplied for this prototype call for a candidate assessment grade that cannot be forcibly downgraded, a 90-day retake interval, ranking informed by verified achievements, and evidence of expert validity, discrimination, reliability and top-k pair quality. The requirements PDF was provided separately from this checkout; this document uses its extracted requirements summary. The following synthetic expectations are **test oracles**, not measured outcomes or claims about real candidates/FSP credentials.

## What exists now and what was actually checked

- [`CareerRoutes.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/CareerRoutes.kt) starts or resumes an attempt of five random active questions (three from the candidate's specialization and two `general` when at least three active specialty items exist, otherwise five `general`), expires open attempts after 30 minutes, checks the latest submitted attempt for a 90-day cooldown **when requesting questions**, scores correct answers as a percentage, and assigns junior `<60`, middle `60–79`, senior `>=80`. Its submission route retains the maximum prior **grade within that specialization**, not necessarily the highest historical score. The profile/matches select the highest-grade attempt, then highest score at that grade. A lower-scoring retake can therefore preserve senior while the reported score/rank changes (for a senior retained on a later junior-scoring attempt, the earlier senior-scoring attempt remains selected). A candidate changing specialization is assessed for the new specialization; no global grade guarantee across specializations is implemented.
- [`V1__career.sql`](../apps/api/src/main/resources/db/migration/V1__career.sql) seeds ten `general` multiple-choice questions; [`V2__candidate_resume.sql`](../apps/api/src/main/resources/db/migration/V2__candidate_resume.sql) adds 15 specialty items (three each for `backend`, `frontend`, `data`, `product`, `design`), for 25 seeded items total, each with three options. The selection route uses three specialty plus two general questions when that specialty has at least three active items; otherwise it falls back to five general. There are no item difficulty/discrimination estimates, independent expert ratings, equating forms, or reliability data. The answer keys are in public server migrations and are not returned by the questions endpoint, but a publicly available seed bank is **not secure assessment content**.
- V2 requires `processingConsent=true` separately from `consentPublication=true` to store a profile and request questions; matches require both consents, a nonblank full name, same specialization, a completed assessment and assessed grade meeting the effective minimum. `DELETE /api/candidates/me/consent` clears profile fields and erases the candidate's attempts/answers, achievements, resume drafts and invitations while leaving an empty account/profile shell. `GET /api/assessments/history` lists the candidate's completed results newest first; these are not a psychometric validation report. [`ResumeRoutes.kt`](../apps/api/src/main/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/ResumeRoutes.kt) accepts one text-extractable PDF at most 5 MB/30 pages with processing consent, stores a suggested-fields draft rather than directly applying parsed content, then requires candidate review/confirmation within one day; confirmation updates selected profile fields, never verified email or publication consent. Do not upload real resumes for the synthetic protocol. `GET /api/candidates/me/resume.pdf` produces a branded Cyrillic PDF from a consented saved profile; it does not issue an official credential.
- Matches require publication consent, processing consent, a nonblank full name, same specialization, a completed assessment and assessed grade meeting the employer's effective minimum. Rank is `selected assessment score + min(sum of verified achievement points, 20) + 5 for equal nonblank industry`, descending, then candidate UUID ascending. Achievement records have server-side `verified_at NOT NULL`; there is no candidate achievement-submission endpoint. The route returns the entire sorted list, **not a paginated top-k endpoint**. Optional `specialization` and `grade` query parameters override employer-need defaults; comma-separated `techStack` requires all requested skills case-insensitively, while `verifiedAchievements=true` requires at least one server-stored verified achievement. `techStack` is self-reported and filters results but contributes no points. `desiredGrade` is survey intent, not the assessed grade.
- The grade-preserving `POST /api/assessments` does **not itself check** 90-day cooldown: that check is in `GET /api/assessments/questions`. Test the boundary as an end-to-end invariant; do not equate a successful GET-only test with enforcement against pre-existing/open attempts, direct submissions, or concurrency. There is no endpoint to select a fixed question set or inject a clock; deterministic outcome tests need an isolated database/clock fixture or a future test seam, not guessed responses to a random draw. `specialization=general` now uses one five-item general draw instead of potentially overlapping three-item/two-item draws; a live regression assertion must still verify five distinct returned IDs.

A second run with `TEST_DATABASE_URL=jdbc:postgresql://localhost:5432/fsp_career` against the local Compose PostgreSQL returned **9 passed, 0 skipped, 0 failed**, including the Flyway/seed/invalid-role constraint integration check; the report was inspected after that run. [`ValidationTest.kt`](../apps/api/src/test/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/ValidationTest.kt) checks input-grade/text validation, salary request deserialization and default invitation contact fields. [`ResumeTest.kt`](../apps/api/src/test/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/ResumeTest.kt) tests extraction of synthetic PDF suggestions, PDF-size/type rejection, career-field validation and Cyrillic PDF export, not HTTP upload/confirm authorization or PDF malware safety. [`DatabaseIntegrationTest.kt`](../apps/api/src/test/kotlin/ru/fsp/career/DatabaseIntegrationTest.kt) checked the 25-question V1+V2 seed count and invalid-role constraint against the local database. These tests do **not** exercise the HTTP assessment/ranking/authorization flows or psychometric quality. A real local database migration/constraint check ran. ## Broader isolated functional evaluation (beyond the completed smoke run)

1. Use a **throwaway PostgreSQL database**, API and Keycloak realm, never real people or production data. Pin repository revision, JDK/Gradle/PostgreSQL versions, DB migration checksum, time zone UTC, API configuration, issued synthetic role-bearing tokens and execution time. Set `TEST_DATABASE_URL`, `TEST_DATABASE_USER`, `TEST_DATABASE_PASSWORD` for the existing migration test, then run `cd apps/api && gradle test --console=plain`; record passes/skips/failures separately. API tests require a running JWT issuer with correct `iss` and `aud`; do not bypass auth or publish access tokens.
2. Create distinct synthetic identities: candidate **A** (`backend`, industry `fintech`, opted in), candidate **B** (`backend`, `fintech`, opted in), candidate **C** (`backend`, not opted in), candidate **D** (`frontend`, opted in), candidate **E** (`backend`, opted in, no assessment), and employer **H** (`backend`, `fintech`, minimum `junior`). Set `processingConsent=true` for A/B/C/D/E; grant `consentPublication` only where explicitly opted in. Give each a nonblank synthetic name; keep contact addresses fictitious and private. Record UUIDs locally, never in public result dumps. Establish completed attempts via legitimate API calls in the isolated instance; because the draw is random and server holds the correct key, record **actual** drawn question IDs and resulting score, rather than assuming a particular draw. For deterministic boundary tests, fixture preparation may insert completed synthetic attempts into test DB in a transaction, with explicit `submitted_at`, `specialization`, `score`, `grade` and five question IDs, solely in that isolated DB; separately validate the HTTP scoring path with normal API submissions. Do not call DB-inserted outcomes evidence that the assessment correctly scored responses.
3. **Grade/retake matrix:** valid five-of-five score 100 → senior; four-of-five 80 → senior; three-of-five 60 → middle; two-of-five 40 → junior. Submit each via a separate synthetic account or reset the isolated fixture. For A, seed an older senior 80 attempt in `backend`, make it more than 90 days old, then submit a 40 retake; expect response `score=40, grade=senior` and profile/matches grade senior with score chosen by the actual highest-grade selection rule (see note above). A failed 40 attempt must not force junior. For cooldown, seed completed attempts at 89 days 23:59:59 and 90 days 00:00:01 relative to a controlled evaluation instant: expect GET questions `409` before and access after the threshold; record timestamps and clock tolerance. Also test cooldown across specializations, repeat/foreign/expired attempt IDs, duplicated/missing answers, invalid options, parallel starts/submissions and attempted direct POST during cooldown. Explicitly record any discrepancy as a failure, not a pass justified by UI prevention. With real wall-clock testing, choose timestamps comfortably inside/outside the boundary instead of claiming second-level precision.
4. **Ranking matrix:** prepare same-specialization assessed A/B with equal scores and industries; verify tie order by UUID. Give A two server-inserted verified achievements worth 12 and 15 points: expect `achievementScore=20`, never 27. Give B no achievement; for the industry test change B's industry to `retail` and H's industry to `retail` while A remains `fintech`: expect B's industry bonus 5 in the explanation, all other score components unchanged. Vary score gaps to cover bonus win/loss/tie, thresholds (`minGrade` middle/senior), C non-publication, D different specialization and E unassessed; C/D/E must not appear with H's `backend` defaults. Check `specialization=frontend` finds D only if assessed; `grade=senior` overrides the need, `techStack=Kotlin,SQL` requires both skills irrespective of casing, and `verifiedAchievements=true` excludes B. Supply oversized/invalid query values and check `400`. Self-reported tech stack should never change total score. Reset filter/fixture states between assertions. Candidate-supplied `grade`, `score`, or an invented achievement in profile JSON must not increase rank or create an achievement row; verify returned profile and database state. The schema cannot store `verified_at=NULL`, so an unverified-achievement ranking test needs a *proposed* pending-submission schema, not a fabricated current-row fixture. Capture complete `/api/matches` ordering, explanatory terms and UUID tiebreak; slice the first k client-side for benchmark purposes and state that this is **not** a server top-k API.
5. **Privacy/security:** bearer absent, bad signature, expired token, wrong issuer/audience, wrong role, foreign candidate attempt, foreign invitation, and candidate-as-employer should fail without data disclosure. Check that `/api/matches` never contains email/phone and that invitation GET/POST returns null contacts before acceptance; only an invited candidate can accept and then both authorized parties can see contact. Review rejection/viewing transitions and logs/errors for leakage. Check email ownership separately: the API derives candidate email from a Keycloak token with `email_verified=true` and rejects mismatched nonblank profile email; test both verified and unverified tokens. Check `PUT` without processing consent fails, and independent publication withdrawal removes discovery without erasing a stored profile. For PDF import, test upload without consent, wrong owner/expired/already-confirmed draft, malformed/encrypted/oversize/non-text PDF and parsed email spoofing; verify only reviewed fields are applied, with no email/publication changes. Delete processing consent and verify profile fields cleared, attempts/history, draft and achievements erased and invitations absent for both parties; verify no match or new invitation remains. This deletes application rows but does not establish complete erasure of backups, Keycloak account, logs or downstream copies; audit those separately. Local Mailpit handles only synthetic development verification, not proof of delivery to real mailboxes or a production security certification.
6. Store a redacted run manifest (commit/revision, migration digest, fixture SQL digest, UTC dates, environment versions), HTTP status/response assertions, test report counts and anonymized ranking output. Destroy the throwaway database after review. If result differs from expected, preserve failing fixture and classify implementation bug versus oracle error. Never publish answers, tokens, contact data, or a real candidate ranking.

## Independent quality study before any consequential use

**Content/expert validity.** Define a job-task blueprint by specialization and seniority with employer and FSP-domain specialists; have at least two independent experts per item rate relevance, factual correctness, grade level, ambiguity and accessibility while blinded to the key and each other's ratings. Adjudicate disagreements and publish versioned item IDs, blueprint coverage by cell, expert agreement (e.g. weighted kappa with uncertainty) and exclusions. Ten general plus fifteen specialty seed questions are insufficient evidence of FSP-specific expertise. Document eligibility and conflicts of interest of judges; a label such as “verified FSP achievement” needs an official issuer verification workflow, not mere DB insertion.

**Discrimination/calibration.** In consented pilots with sufficient sample per specialization and level, estimate per-item facility (`correct/attempted`), corrected item–rest correlation, distractor selection frequencies and grade-band confusion against *independent* expert ratings or task performance. Pre-register sample-size and acceptable thresholds before observation; report confidence intervals, missingness, item exposure and subgroup performance. Remove or revise non-discriminating/leaky items; do not tune on the held-out validation sample. Five items drawn from a public 25-item mixed bank (only three items per implemented specialty) are not interchangeable proven forms.

**Reliability/decision consistency.** Pre-register repeat-test interval, no-feedback condition and independent forms, then compute internal consistency only where model assumptions and sample sizes permit, test–retest agreement (weighted kappa/ICC appropriate to endpoint), classification consistency around 60/80 and uncertainty intervals. Document practice effects, exposure, cooldown impact and whether a preserved maximum grade inflates agreement/false positives. Stratify audit by specialization and consented demographic groups where lawful; suppress small cells and avoid using protected characteristics to score candidates.

**Top-k expert/pair evaluation.** For each held-out employer need, have independent domain judges label *all* eligible synthetic/consented candidates (or a predefined probability sample covering retrieved and unretrieved candidates) for relevance 0–3 and pairwise preference; adjudicate or retain ties/uncertainty. Freeze system version, `k` (e.g. 3 and 5), candidate pool and exclusions before labels are revealed. Evaluate eligibility precision and recall, `precision@k`, `recall@k` against judged-relevant candidates, `nDCG@k` with explicit gain/discount, and pair concordance: among judge-comparable candidate pairs involving at least one system top-k candidate, count system-correct order / all comparable evaluated pairs, excluding expert ties and reporting tie/unknown counts. Report macro average over employer needs, number of needs/candidates/pairs, bootstrap confidence intervals resampling needs, and comparison against score-only and randomized eligible baselines under the same pools. If not all eligible pairs are judged, report judged-pair coverage and sampling weights; never treat unjudged candidates as irrelevant. Separate offline agreement from hiring utility and fairness. **No such judgments, samples, metric values or baselines are available in this repository.**

## Proposed FSP integration boundary (not implemented)

Use an issuer-controlled connector/service account, not candidate-entered points. Proposed `achievement_verifications(id, candidate_id, source='fsp', external_subject_ref, external_achievement_id, achievement_type, issuer, achieved_at, status[pending|verified|revoked], verified_at, revoked_at, evidence_ref, source_version, last_checked_at)` with a uniqueness constraint on `(source,external_achievement_id,candidate_id)`; candidate-to-external-subject binding must be consented and issuer-validated. Keep evidence references restricted, provenance/audit events immutable, and avoid copying sensitive payloads to rank responses. A separately versioned scoring policy maps verified, unrevoked types to capped points; replay-safe imports, revocation refresh, manual review, appeal/correction and consent withdrawal update eligibility. Explicitly distinguish participation, prize and qualification and obtain FSP approval for use of marks/claims. Existing [`achievements`](../apps/api/src/main/resources/db/migration/V1__career.sql) have title, issuer, points and mandatory verified timestamp but no issuer proof, revocation, source ID or candidate identity binding; do not label their rows official FSP achievements without external verification.

## Decision and limitations

The current prototype supports demonstration of intended mechanics, **not** a validated selection instrument. Retake enforcement is limited to the question-start route, exposed public questions are guessable, and no measured validity, reliability, discrimination, employer agreement or top-k quality exists. Keep synthetic demonstrations separate from user-facing credential claims; require the functional security matrix, expert study and independent held-out ranking evaluation before claiming fitness for recruitment decisions.
